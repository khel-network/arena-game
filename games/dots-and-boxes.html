How to Play Dots and Boxes

  1. The board is a grid of dots — on SkillClash, a 4×4 grid (16 boxes).
  2. Players take turns drawing one line between two adjacent dots — horizontally or vertically.
  3. If your line completes the fourth side of a box, you claim that box and go again.
  4. When all 16 boxes are claimed, the player with the most boxes wins.

Dots and Boxes Strategy

Most beginners think Dots and Boxes is about completing boxes. Wrong. It's about forcing your opponent to give you boxes. Here's how the strategy actually works:

1. Never complete the third side of a box

The moment you draw the third side of any box, you hand that box to your opponent — they'll complete it and go again. Avoid this unless you're forced.

2. The chain principle

Late in the game, the board breaks into "chains" — sequences of boxes where completing one triggers the next. Skilled players count the chain lengths and calculate who'll be forced to open the first chain.

3. Sacrifice early, dominate late

It's often correct to give your opponent the first 2-3 boxes if it means you get the last 10. The player who opens the last chain usually loses the board.

4. Force them to open first

Every "safe" move you make reduces the safe moves they have available. Eventually someone has to open a chain. Make sure it's them, not you.
